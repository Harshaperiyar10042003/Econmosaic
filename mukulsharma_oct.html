Nestlé VS Supreme Court of India: Suspension of MFN clause by Switzerland

Economic Times, recently, informed and discussed about the MFN clause scrapping decision by Switzerland government. According to the news, the Switzerland government announced that, "it will suspend the most favoured nation (MFN) clause in its Double Tax Avoidance Agreement (DTAA) with India effective January 1, 2025". This move may raise the tax outgo on dividend income for Indian entities operating in the European country. This is a result of, Nestlé's challenge against the Indian government's decision to impose restrictions on imported products, including testing and quality standards for food items. Nestlé argued these restrictions violated India's WTO obligations, including the MFN principle, as they unfairly targeted foreign companies while benefiting domestic competitors. It serves as an example of how domestic regulations and trade obligations intersects, while it is not directly a WTO MFN dispute. This would especially impact Indian companies having overseas direct investment structures with subsidiaries in Switzerland and will raise the Swiss withholding tax on dividends from 5% to 10% from January 1, 2025.

Switzerland's decision to suspend the unilateral application of the MFN clause under its tax treaty with India, marks a significant shift in bilateral treaty dynamics. Grounded in the Indian apex court's Nestle ruling, which rejected the automatic applicability of MFN clause, this move underscores the growing emphasis on reciprocity and mutual agreement in the interpretation of treaty provisions.

Let me explain, what is MFN?

The Most Favored Nation (MFN) clause is a principle of non-discrimination under the World Trade Organization (WTO) framework. It requires a WTO member to treat all trading partners equally by granting them the same trade benefits as the "most favored" nation. For example, if a country lowers import tariffs for one WTO member, it must extend the same lower tariff to all other members.
