Record high trade deficit and the weakening of Indian Rupee

The Indian Rupee has hit an all-time low of $85.12 on 23 December 2024. RBI has been selling dollars to stabilize it causing a reduction in the foreign exchange reserves which dropped to a six-month low of $652.8 billion. This is due to the record-high trade deficit in India of $37.8 billion during the month of November 2024. Though India has witnessed much higher annual trade deficit in previous years, November 2024 marks the highest recorded monthly trade deficit. This is caused by a high import bill and falling exports. In November, merchandise imports rose by 27% year-on-year, while exports dropped by 4.9%.

The gold imports reached a record high of $14.86 billion, a four-fold increase from $3.44 billion in November, 2023. In November 2024, gold imports made up 21.2% of India's total imports — the first time gold imports has surpassed crude oil imports, making gold the largest single import item. According to Global Trade Research Initiative (GTRI), there are many reasons like growing investment demand for gold, tariff reductions and trade loopholes. The import tariffs declined from 15% to 6% intensifying the gold import. The reduction in tariffs makes domestic gold prices slightly higher than global rates, causing gold to be an attractive long-term investment. Traders also use loopholes and import gold through categories that have lower import duties.

Majority of India's export consists of petroleum products - 14% of the total value. The Petroleum exports fell from $9.54 billion in August 2023 to $5.95 billion in August 2024 - a 37.56% drop, mostly due to the Red Sea crises increasing the transportation costs. Other reasons include fall in demand for petroleum products in key markets like Europe due to a rise in EVs (Electronic Vehicles), increase in number of petroleum refineries around the world increasing its supply more than its demand. Experts say that India's exports are import driven which is why though there has been efforts to reduce import bills, the import has been rising. When the export of petroleum products saw a decline, the exports of the non-petroleum products grew at a steady pace, reflecting a 7.4% growth between November of 2023 and 2024.

One of the other factors adding further pressure on Indian rupee is the appreciation of US dollar due to higher interest rates making dollar more attractive globally. India witnessed significant FII outflows from India in the first half of November 2024, increasing the demand for the US dollar, as investors converted their rupee holdings to dollars, exerting downward pressure on the rupee's value. Other reasons are attributed to the rising crude oil prices leading to increased import costs, global economic slow-down and domestic inflationary pressures.
