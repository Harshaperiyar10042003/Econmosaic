INDIA-RUSSIA TRADE TARGET: $100 BILLION BY 2030

At the 25th India-Russia Intergovernmental Commission on Trade, Economic, Scientific, Technological, and Cultural Cooperation (IRIGC-TEC) in New Delhi, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar reaffirmed the strategic importance of bilateral trade. Following disruptions from EU sanctions on Russia due to the Ukraine conflict, both nations aim to elevate their trade target from $30 billion by 2025 to $100 billion by 2030, with transactions prioritized in local currencies.

In FY 2023-24, bilateral trade reached an all-time high of $65.7 billion. Indian exports, valued at $4.26 billion, focused on pharmaceuticals, organic chemicals, electrical machinery, and steel, while imports from Russia, amounting to $61.44 billion, were dominated by oil, fertilizers, minerals, and precious metals. Russian investments in India are concentrated in oil, gas, and petrochemicals, while India's investments in Russia focus on oil and gas sectors.

However, India faces a significant trade deficit of $57 billion, primarily driven by crude oil imports. This imbalance, coupled with geopolitical sanctions on Russia, poses challenges for Indian businesses navigating the risks of secondary sanctions.

To mitigate these challenges, both countries are leveraging mechanisms such as special rupee-vostro accounts and exploring a Free Trade Agreement (FTA).

Mr Jaishankar also highlighted India's expanding role in global energy markets, including oil, gas, coal, and uranium, reflecting the strategic synergy between the two nations.

This partnership underscores a commitment to bolstering trade resilience, addressing imbalances, and fostering deeper economic cooperation amidst evolving global dynamics.
