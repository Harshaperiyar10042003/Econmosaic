Trump Returns: Shaping Inflation and Markets in US Economy

Donald Trump's return to the presidency is expected to bring significant shifts in the U.S. economy. His policies on taxation, trade, and fiscal spending are likely to influence inflation levels and market trends. Trump's 2.0 shapes the expectations of the investors and US is likely to break the traditional ways of governance and policy making. Trump's potential extension of the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) could turn up federal deficits but encourage the short-term corporate growth. Corporate tax rates from 21% to as low as 15% and reinstating full depreciation for capital expenses and encourage investment, temporarily boost stock markets, and increase disposable income for businesses and households.

In contrast to tax policy, Trump's proposals to impose a 60% tariff on Chinese goods and potentially a universal 10% tariff may negatively impact economic growth and put upward pressure on Inflation. Specifically these measures could cause inflation to increase by 2.5% and GDP to decline by 0.5% in the upcoming two years. However Trump's full agenda is still unclear.

Deregulation could be a major theme of a second Trump term. Deregulation aims the major sectors benefitting include are Energy, Manufacturing and Finance. Additionally, pharmaceuticals, cryptocurrencies and blockchain may see favourable treatment from a Trump administration. Reduced federal scrutiny could support broader tech and AI industries. Trump's fiscal policies, including infrastructure spending and tax cuts, could increase bond yields and reduce bond prices, as rising inflation and deficits pressure fixed-income investments. While these policies may spur near-term economic growth, they could have long-term implications for the economy's stability.

Ultimately, Trump's second term would involve balancing economic growth with the risks of deficits, inflation and market changes. Investors, policymakers, and consumers will need to navigate these complexities as the administration moves forward.
