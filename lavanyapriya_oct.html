MALAYSIA’S ECONOMY EXCEEDS EXPECTATIONS IN Q3 2024

As the world economy continues to tread through uncertainty, Malaysia has emerged as an island of resilience and dynamism. Barring that from market expectations, Malaysia's economy posted a more-than-decent performance in the third quarter of 2024. It is worth saying that the achievement lies not only in good policymaking but also in the spirit of industry and workforce. Malaysia’s growth GDP of 2023 is 3.68% a decline of 4.97% from 2022 GDP (8.65%). Malaysia’s Gross Domestic Product (GDP) grew 5.3% from 4.8% in the third quarter of this year (Q3 2024). Malaysia’s growth increased by increase in investment and by steady rise in exports. Malaysia’s Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim had previously forecast Malaysia’s GDP to grow between 4.8 to 5.3 percent which is stronger than the 4.0 to 5.0 percent range in 2023. Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) Governor Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour also attributed the growth to expansion in household spending as well as favorable labour market conditions and Government policy support. Abdul Rasheed Ghaffour said in the external sector, export continued to strengthen on the back of recovering external demand and positive spillovers from the global tech upgrade. He noted that stable inflation rate is 1.9% for both headline and core inflation due to growth to expansion in household spending and favorable labour market conditions and Government policy support. He cautioned that inflation could be still influenced by Government policies, Global commodity price and financial market development. The Central Bank reported that the ringgit strengthen by 14.9% against the US dollar with its Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER) increasing by 9.9%. Governor Rasheed explained that this recovery was partly driven by US Federal Reserve’s move towards easing monetary policy, which eased pressure on regional currencies, including the ringgit. After hitting a 26-year low in February, the ringgit has regained about 2% against the US dollar this year. Rasheed noted that between October 11 and November 13, the ringgit weakened by 7.8% against the US dollar due to the latter’s stronger performance during this time. The Malaysian government forecasts economic growth of 4.5% to 5.5% in 2025, surpassing the 4.6% growth projected by Bloomberg analysts. Increased salaries for civil servants and plans to raise the minimum wage in the private sector are expected to drive stronger domestic demand in the coming year.
