CLIMATE FINANCE: CONCERNS RAISED BY GLOBAL SOUTH RELATING TO CARBON TRADE

After the 29th edition of Conference of Parties (COP) leads as a trending issue between Global north and Global south countries. UN's climate conference ended on 22nd November which was started on 11th November addressed the issue of carbon emission.

The main objective of COP29 was the New Collective Quantified Goal (NCQG) involves the transfer of money from developed to developing countries to achieve their goals (i.e) to reduce the usage and curb the emission of greenhouse gases. Developed countries have transferred about 115 billion dollars in 2021-2022 to meet Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC) but the developing countries have been expecting the value to be "trillions of dollars" which continues to be a sharp split between them.

The NDCs are the voluntary plans done by all the counties with a target to reduce carbon emission by certain quantity before 2030 and it is crucial to note that even if all countries fulfilled their voluntary commitments it would cut only about 2% while the recent report of 2023 shows nearly 0.8% of carbon emission. The demanded money was not only to meet (NDC) but also to work against climate change and to make up for all climate damages done earlier.

The Developed countries led by EU says that these demands are high and they wanted collective work from all actors to hike up climate finance to $1.3 trillion every year till 2035. They agreeing to take the lead have a set of goals to contribute $250-300 billion by 2035 each year which consists of a "Variety of sources" including public and private, bilateral and multilateral, and alternative sources.

China had petitioned the Presidency of COP29 which was primarily directed at a EU proposal called the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) which imposes tax on products imported into the EU that don't conform to carbon-emission norms required by the union. The first day of the conference saw an agreement on carbon markets to be supervised by the UN because such markets allow countries to trade carbon credits among themselves whose prices are determined as a consequence of emission caps. This market follows the Article 6 of Paris Agreement which justify how countries can bilaterally trade carbon among themselves participating in a global carbon market which was greatly criticised by Environmentalists world wide.
