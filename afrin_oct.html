Ethiopia's economic reforms boost SME growth, innovation

A view of the workshop    SOURCE: THE ETHIOPIAN HERALD TUESDAY 5 NOVEMBER 2024

The Ethiopian Herald is government owned English language newspaper published by Ethiopian Press Agency. On 5th November 2024 they published a news article related to Ethiopia's Small-Medium Enterprises growth. News addresses the workshop and meeting conducted by the Ethiopian Intellectual Property Authority (EIPA) on 4th and 5th November 2024 in ADDIS ABABA (The capital city of Ethiopia). Ethiopia's homegrown economic and macroeconomic reforms stimulate to have a supportive environment for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs), according to the Ethiopian Intellectual Property Authority (EIPA). In collaboration with World Intellectual Property organization (WIPO) and Japan Patent Office (JPO) held a National Intellectual Property workshop in-order to increase SME's awareness of innovation management and importance of intellectual property (IP) which means a right's such as patents, trademarks, and copyrights offer a means of protecting innovation, attracting investment plans and expecting market access both locally and globally.

In a meeting EIPA Director- General Woldu Yimsel stated that, "These economic reforms give backing to the way for SMEs to play active role in Ethiopia's economy their current contribution to the GDP remains modest". Aimed to boost awareness and create forum for discussing SME-related agendas. Woldu pointed out that many developed countries like Japan, Taiwan and South Korea have grown their economy through active SME sector and greater contribution to GDP and employment opportunity. WIPO's Division for Africa Acting Director Loretta Aseidu stressed that, "SMEs are crucial for driving economic growth through innovation and creativity". She cited ILO data indicating that, "SMEs employ over two-thirds of the global workforce play a major role in GDP contributions, poverty reduction, and promotes equality by creating productive employment". And underscored the importance of IP bring up growth and providing a competitive edge for SMEs. This meeting also served as an avenue to learn from international best practices through IP, SMEs can turn knowledge and creativity into tangible economic assets that support business transformation. The meeting helped to expand promotion of IP and facilitated discussions with policy makers and stakeholders and the event undergone during Ethiopia was ratifying the Paris Industrial Property Convention and the Madrid International Trademark Registration Protocol.
