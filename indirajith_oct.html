Myanmar's Crazy Currency System

In Myanmar, the official currency, the ''Kyat'' (symbolized as "K"), is colloquially pronounced as "Chat." Due to the weakened value of the Kyat, people often conduct transactions in U.S. dollars, receiving change in Kyat. This practice highlights the Kyat's diminished purchasing power. Since the military coup in February 2021, Myanmar's economy has been grappling with severe challenges under the control of the ''Tatmadaw'' (Myanmar's military forces). Key issues include: Lack of Government Initiative: There are few policies or initiatives addressing economic recovery. Frozen Foreign Reserves: Approximately $1 billion in U.S. banks and $4.5 billion in Singapore remain inaccessible to Myanmar, limiting economic stability. Petroleum Shortage: Petroleum resources are depleted, and China and Thailand have reduced their imports, cutting a significant revenue stream. Startup Stagnation: Economic instability has halted new business ventures and startup growth. Rising Inflation: From 2022 to 2023, the cost of goods surged by 27%, reducing affordability and driving emigration as citizens seek better financial security abroad. To counter these issues, the Tatmadaw has implemented strict foreign exchange policies, mandating that businesses convert 25% of their foreign income to Kyat and that exporters convert remaining earnings post-export. However, experts like Jared Bissinger argue that these funds barely cover military expenses, with much of the revenue reportedly funneled towards the Tatmadaw's interests rather than public welfare. To stabilize the economy, experts suggest reducing the Tatmadaw's budget and investing in reforms such as interest rate liberalization, foreign exchange policy adjustments, and inflation control.

DID YOU KNOW?

Myanmar supplies 90% of the world's jade, making it a leading exporter of this valuable natural resource.
