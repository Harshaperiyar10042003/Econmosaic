Warning Lights: Bioluminescence in Chennai as a Sign of Ecological Distress

Image source: The Economics Times

The flowering of marine plankton Noctiluca scintillans on Chennai's beaches in October is indeed a beautiful sight, yet it is an equally disturbing phenomenon. The oceans glowing in blue is an attractive spectacle, But scientists suggest that such blooms may indicate that something is wrong in the environment. These blooms are well known to flourish in nutrient over rich without oxygen which is usually due to pollution sources such as sewage, industrial run-offs, and also global warming of the ocean waters. In the case of Chennai, the phytoplankton bloom can be associated with the increase of nutrient content into the coastal waters with the help of heavy rains.

From an environmental economics perspective, this bioluminescence event reflects a negative externality—where the economic activities causing pollution do not bear the full cost of the environmental damage. Such blooms, if they persist, can be harmful to local fisheries by causing the water oxygen level to reduce and causing disturbance to the marine food webs which may cause fish deaths. It impacts not only the biodiversity but also the local community which depends on fisheries. In addition, these incidents emphasize the significance of having management plans in coastal zones and resources and effective waste disposal, control of pollution and conservations of marine ecosystems.
